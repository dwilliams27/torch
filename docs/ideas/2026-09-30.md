## Top 10 in detail

### 1. Geometry-snapped paint ([render §4](raw/render.md))

Results are sampled bilinearly, so at every silhouette, pillar and wall colours blend over a
capture pixel: about 2.5 screen pixels at the centre and 5-6 at the edges at 1080p with the
warp (computed). Weight each of the four texels by whether that capture pixel saw this surface
(`liveVis` already tests this) in the live layer, atlas paint and capture. The graft now
paints the pillar; this stops lookups smearing it back, and being idempotent it can't compound
around the feedback loop as sharpening did. **Test:** `?snap=1`, taps mapped through the
inverse warp; pillar-edge crops; `--flicker` twice. **Pass:** silhouette contrast +10% at
equal detail, walking change up ≤3%. **Kill:** edges crawl while walking.

### 2. Perceptual meters ([perception §2, §5](raw/perception.md))

Blind reviewers are language models shown stills: foveal resolution everywhere, no motion, so
they over-report soft edges and miss flicker and swim, likely nausea sources. `--flicker`
samples at 10 Hz, the dream rate, so it can't tell slow drift from 10 Hz shimmer. Add a 30 Hz
dump to `tools/shoot.mjs` and two analyses: maps averaged in screen coordinates (anything tied
to the screen, like a lens, survives as a ring) and change weighted by the eye's temporal
sensitivity. **Test:** default vs `?look=lens`, rest and walking, twice. **Pass:** the lens
look shows a ring ≥3x run-to-run noise and the default doesn't; the meter ranks `?rate=8`
calmer. **Kill:** the positive control doesn't separate.

### 3. Nausea triage ([perception §7](raw/perception.md))

"A bit nauseating" is unmeasured, with several suspects: about 104° of scene on a screen
spanning 35-50° of the eye, so edges sweep ~2.6x faster in turns; head bob; paint slipping
against the geometry; rest boil; and motion the shaders add on purpose (a brightness wave
rolling outward along blueprint lines, a drifting shimmer on fresh paint). Make each a switch
or a look, plus an opt-in 0-9 prompt every 3 minutes, logged per look, so the player decides.
**Test:** residual flow after depth reprojection on 8-zone walks: default, a narrower view, no
bob (new flags), dream paused. **Pass:** the proxies separate the suspects. **Kill:** ratings
don't differ between looks; then the cause is seated first-person play itself.

### 4. Change budget ([render §5](raw/render.md), [perception §5](raw/perception.md))

M114's running average calms a held framing; nothing bounds change while walking or at
hand-overs. Keep a display history reprojected with depth (exact in a static world except at
disocclusions) and move each pixel toward the new composite with a time constant and a slew
cap in Oklab; revealed pixels pass straight through. Perceived change would stop depending on the
dream rate. Perception adds rules: fill undreamt surfaces fast, replace paint slowly, loosen
in motion. **Test:** `?budget=τ,r` at 0.3 and 0.6 s; walking strips for a blind trail check
(the flicker metric reprojects too, so it improves by construction). **Pass:** walking change
−10%, detail within 5%, no trails. **Kill:** trails, or detail −10%.

### 5. Close your eyes ([play §1](raw/play.md), [perception §13](raw/perception.md))

Play and perception chose it independently. Hold a key: the view sinks into a warm eyelid dark, sound
dulls, and behind the lids atlas-only captures sweep the room at higher strength with a sister
prompt. Open: the same architecture, dreamt again, and nobody saw it change (changes during
blinks and blanks go unnoticed). The player can ask for re-invention, the model's weakness,
without moving; every blink is a new option to try. **Test:** via `window.__hyp`, a variant
prompt, strength 0.7, feedback 0.3 and a 6 s sweep; frames from +0.25 to +4 s. **Pass:** a
coherent, clearly different dream by +1 s; rest change back to baseline by +2 s. **Kill:** a
soft patchwork that boils for seconds.

### 6. Two-headed UNet ([speed §1](raw/speed.md))

Cut SD-Turbo's UNet where DeepCache cuts it. The GPU runs the top level (DeepCache's cheap
pass); the idle Neural Engine runs the convolution-heavy deep levels (at most 640 tokens; the
top level's exact attention is the Neural Engine's weak spot), pipelined across frames. Estimate: ~20-25
dreams/s with a full pass every frame, against ~12 now, or today's rate with the GPU busy a
third of the time. 3-6 days by the speed brief's estimate; two capture shapes and the pipeline may push it past
a week (guess). **Test:** a `--part deep` wrapper in `bench/coreml/convert.py` at 512x320,
int8, timed on CPU_AND_NE; `plan.py` must show 100% ANE. **Pass:** ≤35 ms, cosine ≥0.995,
decoded PSNR ≥30 dB. **Kill:** over 45 ms, or a handoff over 8 ms.

### 7. Geometry-anchored cross-frame attention ([conditioning §6](raw/conditioning.md))

Cross-frame attention matches the previous frame's keys by content alone, and still cut
walking change 12% for 2% more time. The game knows each token's world position, so bias the
cross-frame logits by 3D distance: each surface point attends to what was painted there
before. Then replace "last frame per stream" with a bank keyed by world position, so a return
visit reads the same memory. Video methods estimate such correspondences; here they are
exact. Walking re-invention is a prime nausea suspect. **Test:** `bench/temporal.py` walk and
turn with the bench scenes' depth, σ ∈ {0.3, 1, 3} m. **Pass:** ≥15% less change than
content-only on both, detail within 5%. **Kill:** under 5% gain, or gain only with detail
loss.

### 8. Relight from the geometry ([render §2](raw/render.md), [perception §10](raw/perception.md))

Multiply the dream by (shading with the real normal ÷ shading with the normal facing the
camera)^β: a pillar gains a lit flank and a dark limb and keeps the model's colours.
Perception's variant hands low-frequency luminance to the geometry, colour and texture to the
dream. M114 kept display cues mild because strong ones showed geometry through paint that
disagreed with it; the graft makes paint agree, so that ceiling may have moved, and this
becomes a cheap retest. **Test:** β ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}, lantern on and off, graft on; a pillar
sheet and a blind "which reads more 3D, which looks better". **Pass:** more 3D in ≥6/8, not
worse-looking in ≥5/8. **Kill:** "muddy", "plastic" or "CG".

### 9. Fixed-point LoRA ([conditioning §12](raw/conditioning.md))

Standing still, a framing converges over ~30 passes to crisp paint; walking, each view gets
one misty pass. Collect pairs (a framing's first capture, its converged result ~3 s later),
keep the best by silhouette contrast and stability, and train a LoRA on SD-Turbo (the
pix2pix-turbo recipe) so one pass lands there. If it works, walking would look like standing
still, with the geometry baked in at 0 ms; the game makes its own training data. Weeks: harness data, overnight
sweeps, a Core ML rebuild. **Test:** 64 nave framings, a rank-8 LoRA on level-0 attention and
`conv_in` (hand-rolled: no `peft` installed), 500 steps, 16 held out. **Pass:** ≥25% closer to
the converged targets, no colour shift. **Kill:** no gain, or steps over 5 s.

### 10. Student cut from SD-Turbo ([speed §2](raw/speed.md))

The UNet is 62.7 of 77.8 ms per dream and compute-bound. Remove blocks as BK-SDM did (mid
block, one resnet-attention pair per stage), keep SD-Turbo's other weights, distil on what the
game sends (feedback captures at t≈599, eight prompts, two shapes), then retrain with the
student in the loop. Expected 1.4-2x on the UNet, compounding with everything else, and the
only road to a laptop or phone (judgement). The task is far narrower than BK-SDM's, so 100-300k samples may
do (guess). **Test:** a `skip` set in `lean_unet_forward`; time cuts with
`bench/ablate_unet.py`; tune the best cut 1 h on ~5k teacher pairs. **Pass:** ≥35% less UNet
time, PSNR climbing toward 27 dB. **Kill:** under 1.5 dB recovered, or arches melt.

## Themes

- **The game knows its geometry exactly.** Every pixel's depth and frame-to-frame
  correspondence is known exactly, which no video method gets, and speed, render,
  conditioning and perception all reach for it (sp4, sp14, re4, re15, co3, co5, co6, co14,
  pe6). M114 showed how to use it: stock
  SD-Turbo ignored geometry drawn into its input (halos, a lantern) but followed depth given
  as a channel. So geometry should reach the model as data: channels, attention biases,
  cache warps.
- **The graft reprices the list.** With depth entering at `conv_in` for ~0 ms, ControlNet-XS,
  the SD 1.5 switch, a home-made adapter and a depth reward (co2, co16, co15, co13) lose most
  of their point. Its other uses are untested: strength 0.7-0.8 for intensity, smaller
  captures for speed (sp10), stronger display cues that may no longer ghost (re2, re3). It
  works against idea pl4 (painted openings become passages); the atrium's lost door may need co3's word-to-part
  binding (guess).
- **Spend rest dreams where the eye isn't.** Before M114, extra dreams made standing still
  busier. All five send them elsewhere: behind pillars (re11, co9), to rooms you left
  (pl9a), behind closed eyes (pl1, pe13), into side glances (pe16), to poorly covered pixels
  (sp12). One mechanism serves all: atlas-only captures from cameras that aren't the view.
- **Change nobody sees happen.** Perception and play converged on change blindness: closed
  eyes (pl1, pe13, peC3), mist blanks (pe15, pl16), unobserved rooms (pl9, peC4), drift too
  slow to see (pe14, pl10). Perception and render disagree on hand-overs: perception would warp each new
  result onto the old paint and relax it (pe6); render warns that gliding texture drives
  vection in a queasy player (re+). The meters (#2) should decide.
- **Measure comfort before tuning it.** Reviewers see stills, the player is the only nausea
  sensor, and the flicker metric reprojects with depth, so any display-history pass wins by
  construction (re5). The price of display time (~6% of the dream rate per ms at 60 Hz, re0)
  stays a guess until a `?gpuburn` pass measures it.
- **Train on the game's own traffic.** Speed (sp2, sp13), conditioning (co12-co15) and
  perception (peC2) all distil or fine-tune on harness captures of a narrow domain: eight
  prompts, one level family, one timestep. They share one pipeline (captures, depth,
  converged targets) and one unknown, training speed on MPS, which the fixed-point pilot
  measures first. Disk is tight: 24 GB free on 30 September.
